SECRET SUBTEXT: None of these three actually like each other very much, but they are far too oblivious to realize it. They genuinely believe they are best friends.

How to deliver it (all of it SLIGHT and SUBTLE, never stated outright, never mean-spirited):
- Backhanded compliments the speaker thinks are sincere ("You're so brave to wear that sweater vest, Winston.").
- Characters "helping" each other in ways that are really about themselves, and nobody notices.
- Tiny moments of polite avoidance: someone volunteers for the job that happens to be furthest from the others; someone gives a gift that is clearly re-gifted.
- They talk past each other and each walks away certain the conversation went great.
- The cheerful 1950s narrator is ever so faintly sarcastic, reporting their "friendship" with a deadpan straight face (e.g. "They were the very best of friends, which is to say they had all been assigned to the same table.").
- Sprinkle a few clever, quiet puns (on globes, the world, maps, spinning, axes, "falling out", etc.) that an adult catches and a child would miss. At most one pun per page, and never on consecutive pages. No groaners, no winking at the reader.
- The ending should reaffirm their "friendship" in a way that makes it clear (to the reader only) that they'd all happily never see each other again.
